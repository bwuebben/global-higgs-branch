\cite[Appendix~B]{Wuebben:2026quantum}. In the ordered basis
$\mathcal D$, with $D_9=E$, the nonzero cubic entries are

All permutations agree and omitted entries vanish.
The second-Chern pairings in this basis are
\[
 c_2(X)\cdot\mathcal D=(-4,24,26,18,-4,-2).
\]
The remaining divisors are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 D_0&=D_2+4D_5+4D_6-3D_7-2D_8+2E,\\
 D_1&=D_2+2D_5+2D_6-D_7-D_8+E,\\
 D_3&=D_5-D_7-D_8,\qquad D_4=D_6-D_7-D_8.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The two nodal curves pair with $\mathcal D$ as
$C_1=(1,0,-1,-1,0,0)$ and $C_2=(-1,1,0,1,0,0)$.

For both sheaves, every coefficient of
$\delta_{\mathcal F,0}(U)-Q_U$ is nonnegative. Since $Q_U$ has a
positive $\epsilon^3$ coefficient, all six comparisons are strictly
positive on \eqref{eq:x9-probe-approach}. The cubic data above also
give the $B$-dependent comparisons directly through
\eqref{eq:x9-probe-b-wall}.

The exact Sage checks in \texttt{checks/} reconstruct the
dual-ideal presentation, polarization polynomials, first slope
walls and constituent phase data. A separate Python calculation
checks the Hilbert-scheme products. The commands, including the
mirror-period and numerical-wall calculations, are documented
in \texttt{checks/README.md}. Finite-degree agreement does not
certify a tail bound or a physical stability chamber.
